Why Our Oceans Matter

The ocean covers most of our planet and shapes the air we breathe, the climate we live in, and the food that feeds billions of people. It is home to an extraordinary range of life, much of which we are still discovering. Sea turtles cross entire oceans to return to the beach where they hatched. Whales sing across hundreds of kilometres of open water. Coral reefs, built by tiny animals over thousands of years, shelter a quarter of all marine species. Mangrove forests and seagrass meadows quietly nurse the next generation of fish while storing carbon underground. A few groups worth knowing:

The Threats We Face

Small Changes Add Up
to Big Impact

Plastic bags and fishing nets left in the water are mistaken for food and can trap turtles, whales, and seabirds. Overfishing and bycatch put pressure on species that take decades to recover. Coastal construction destroys nesting beaches and mangrove nurseries, and warming, more acidic seas bleach the coral reefs that a quarter of marine life depends on. None of these threats are irreversible — but they need people who notice, speak up, and change small daily habits.

Regional Spotlight

Oman's Coastline: A Closer Look

Oman's coast stretches for thousands of kilometres along the Arabian Sea and the Gulf of Oman, and it holds some of the richest marine life in the region. Green turtles return every year to nest on the beaches of Ras Al Hadd, one of the most important nesting grounds in the Indian Ocean. Coral gardens shelter reef fish around the Daymaniyat Islands, mangrove forests along Qurm nurse fish and birds, and gentle dugongs graze on seagrass meadows at Barr Al Hikman found nowhere else in the Gulf.

  • Ras Al Hadd — major green turtle nesting beach
  • Daymaniyat Islands — protected coral reef reserve
  • Barr Al Hikman — seagrass meadows & dugong habitat
  • Qurm Nature Reserve — mangrove forest & bird habitat

How You Can Help

♻️

Reduce Single-Use Plastic

Carry a reusable bottle and bag, and skip plastic straws and cutlery. Plastic that reaches the sea is one of the biggest threats to turtles, whales, and seabirds everywhere.

🐟

Choose Sustainable Seafood

Ask where your fish comes from and favour species that are line-caught rather than netted. Buying sustainably helps keep fish stocks and reef ecosystems in balance.

📣

Report Sightings & Speak Up

If you spot a turtle nest, an injured animal, or illegal dumping, report it to your local environmental authority. Awareness spreads fastest when you talk about it too.

🌱

Support Protected Areas

Marine reserves like the Daymaniyat Islands give ecosystems room to recover. Support organisations and policies that fund and enforce these protections.
